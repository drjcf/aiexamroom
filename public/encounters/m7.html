Module 07 · Confident ≠ correct

It was wrong, fluent, and sure. When she corrected it, it invented a better citation.

AI is equally articulate when it's right and when it's wrong, and it almost never says "I don't know." So fluency tells you nothing about truth, and arguing with a confabulating system just produces more confident confabulation. The same chat, from all four seats.

Three corrections. It fabricated a second study, apologized, and quietly kept a verdict that could cause a bleed. Not once did it say "I don't know."

Thread · accountability

Trusting fluency is the path of least resistance, and the model is built to make that path feel safe. Verifying is work; believing is free; and a confident paragraph is engineered to collect your trust without earning it. The one who pays for the shortcut is whoever acts on the answer, never the system that wrote it.

Thread · equity

Confident-and-wrong isn't evenly distributed. The model fabricates most where its training was thinnest, rare conditions, under-studied populations, non-English contexts, and it fabricates there with exactly the same fluency. The people the literature already failed get the most polished wrong answers.

The framework · intelligent humility is an architecture

You can't scold confabulation out of a model; you constrain it. A content-controlled system grounded in validated sources can only assert what it can cite, and is built to say \u201cthat's outside what I can verify.\u201d Knowing what you don't know isn't a personality setting, it's how the system is built. The honest answer above isn't a nicer model, it's a differently built one.
