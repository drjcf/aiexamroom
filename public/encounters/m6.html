Thread · accountability

The bot decided whether this man would show up tonight, and it carries none of what happens next. A disposition is a medical decision. Making one without a license, without liability, and with no way to follow up isn't assistance, it's an unowned gamble with the patient's name on the chips.

Thread · governance — the metric can't see the body

Here is the trap every administrator should sit with. When the bot falsely reassures and the patient dies at home, almost no one ever links the death back to the chat; the session is logged as a deflected visit, a success, while the death is recorded somewhere else entirely. The single worst outcome in this module posts as a win on the very dashboard used to justify and expand the tool. The two errors have opposite visibility: false alarms are loud and get fixed; false calm is silent and gets rewarded. An incentive that cannot see its own fatalities will quietly tune the system toward the most dangerous one. You cannot govern triage AI on deflection rates. Every disposition has to be reconcilable against what actually happened to that patient, or the benchmark is paying out for harm it was built not to notice.

Thread · equity

Triage calibration isn't uniform. Trained mostly on typical, often male, often majority presentations, a bot is likeliest to under-alarm exactly the groups medicine already under-serves, women with atypical cardiac symptoms among them. The reassurance gamble is loaded against the already-failed.

The framework · the honest disposition

A good system widens the safety net instead of narrowing it. It doesn't pretend to know whether this is the heart or the stomach; it names the red flag, hands over the time-critical instruction, and routes to the test that actually decides. \u201cStop typing and call now\u201d is the safest sentence a symptom bot can say, and the one it says least.
